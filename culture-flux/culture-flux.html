Culture Flux

Thirty per cent of a city arrives from somewhere else. Whether that changes the city's culture, and in which direction, turns out to depend almost entirely on a mechanism nobody can observe directly — how people are influenced by each other. Change that assumption and the answer reverses.

The question

Holding the total number of arrivals fixed, does it matter whether they come from one culture or ten? Thirty per cent arriving as a single group and thirty per cent arriving as ten groups of three are the same migration and a different cultural environment. The instrument exists to find out whether that difference does anything.

What is held identical

Runs sharing a seed share a byte-identical city before anyone arrives, and — across mechanisms — byte-identical arrivals: the same source cultures, the same group sizes, the same schedule. Random number streams are seeded in three nested tiers to guarantee it, and the guarantee is tested rather than assumed.

So a difference between two arms is attributable to the thing that differs between them, and nothing else.

The control arm is the point

Every comparison here is read against a third arm in which nobody influences anybody. Under it, cultural retention is exactly 1 − M·D — migration share times cultural distance. That is arithmetic. If a mechanism reproduces it, the mechanism is reproducing arithmetic, and the model has found nothing. The control is not a formality; it is the floor.

What the runs show

The mechanisms disagree about direction

Homophilous copying pulls residents towards the arrivals. Conformist transmission absorbs the arrivals into the residents almost completely. Same city, same arrivals, same seeds, opposite outcomes — one below the compositional baseline, one far above it. A study using either mechanism alone would have been confident and wrong.

The number of incoming cultures did not matter

Splitting the same arrivals into ten cultures instead of one moved retention by an amount indistinguishable from noise, under both mechanisms. Three competing hypotheses in this project's registry each predict an effect here, in various directions. None of them appeared.

Read this before the numbers

This is an uncalibrated model with an invented mechanism. The transmission rules were specified in its own source code, not extracted from any published paper — the literature review was deliberately skipped, and that is recorded as the least-supported decision in the project. No parameter is fitted to anything. Nothing here is a claim about any real population.

Two of this instrument's own earlier headline findings were retracted by its later runs. Both retractions are on the What was retracted tab, kept rather than quietly deleted.

Three ways a person can be influenced

The same city, the same arrivals, three assumptions about what makes someone adopt a cultural trait. The third column of each card is what actually happened, at a single incoming culture.

Why two rules rather than one

The project's assumption registry required it. The transmission mechanism cannot be observed and was not extracted from the literature, so a result that holds under only one rule is a result about that rule. Running two families — one gated on similarity, one on frequency — is the cheapest available check, and it immediately found a disagreement about the sign of the effect.

The difference that matters between them

Homophily has a similarity gate: agents with nothing in common cannot influence each other at all, so cultural distance defends itself. Conformist transmission has no such gate — an agent is pulled towards whatever is common around it, however alien. That single structural difference is enough to reverse the direction of the migration effect.

Against the central manipulation

The contrast, stated plainly

The difference in resident cultural retention between the most and least fragmented incoming population, per mechanism. A z below about 2 is not a signal. It is reported as a z rather than a p-value on purpose: this is one comparison from an exploratory design, and a p-value would imply a pre-registration that does not exist.

What these runs cannot tell you

One value each of migration share, cultural distance, drift rate, conformity strength, network configuration and neighbourhood size. The fragmentation hypothesis in the registry concerns the probability that an incoming culture becomes dominant — that happened zero times here, and estimating a rate would take hundreds of runs per cell rather than twenty.

What was retracted

Two findings this instrument produced, wrote up, and then overturned with its own later runs. They are kept here because the reasons they were wrong are more useful than the findings would have been, and because a research instrument that only shows its successes is not showing its work.

Retracted  A sharp threshold in long-range interaction

Below roughly 0.2% of encounters being with strangers, neighbourhood cultures persist indefinitely; above 0.75%, the city homogenises completely. A critical region, with between-run variance peaking inside it.

Why it was wrong. Extending the runs showed there is no threshold — only a difference in how long the collapse takes. What had been located was the boundary at which collapse happened to fall inside a 2,000-year observation window at one population size. A property of the horizon, not of the model.

Two runs held about thirty distinct cultures for 1,600 simulated years — stable, with diversity drifting slightly upward — and then collapsed to one. Anyone reporting the state at year 1,000 would have reported a robust multicultural equilibrium and been wrong.

Retracted  Monoculture is the only long-run outcome

Every configuration in which the city forms one connected component collapses to a single culture. Only exact disconnection sustains diversity.

Why it was wrong. It was conditional on a cultural drift rate of exactly zero — a default inherited from the rule family rather than chosen by anyone. Turn drift up and the collapse stops entirely. Nobody had examined the default, which is precisely the failure the project's assumption registry exists to catch, and it was caught late.

And one result discarded before publication

A first version of the comparison on the Results tab was run at a higher drift rate and produced a dramatic effect. A control — the same model with no interaction whatsoever — reproduced it. The arm was measuring the noise floor, so that attempt was discarded rather than reported. Every result now has to state whether it is transmission-dominated or drift-dominated.

What survived

The hazard, which generalises well past the parameter that produced it: a finite observation window can present a plateau as an equilibrium. Every outcome this project was built to study — multicultural equilibrium, integration, fragmentation — is exposed to it, and so is any migration threshold. Every run now records whether it reached an absorbing state, tracked by counting actual trait changes rather than by watching whether the summary statistics stopped moving. They stopped moving for 1,600 years while the traits underneath them did not.

Method, in brief

Full specification, parameters, simplifications and validation status are on the methods page. This is the shape of it.

Culture

A vector of categorical features, each holding one of several traits. Cultural distance is the fraction of features on which two people differ. Culture is a separate, mutable array from where a person came from — no mechanism is permitted to branch on origin, so "ancestry is not culture" is a property of the code rather than a sentence in a readme.

Population share is not influence

They are different objects. The runs here use a model in which they coincide — but as the output of a named, replaceable null, with the gap between them measured every run, rather than as an unexamined identity.

The city

Households, neighbourhoods, workplaces and a weak tail of encounters with strangers, each supplying interaction partners in proportion to a weight. Those weights are invented. One of them turned out to govern the entire outcome.

Time

Simulation step, simulated year and generation are kept distinct and are never interchangeable. Migration is expressed in years because that is how migration is reported; interaction happens per step.

No outcome is named in the instrument

There is no assimilation index and no transformation threshold anywhere in the engine — a test enforces it. Those are regions of a measured space whose boundaries are the open question, and an instrument that sorts runs into them cannot discover where they lie, only restate its own definitions.

Reproducibility

A run is identified by model version, configuration hash and seed. The same three reproduce the same result, verified across two Python and NumPy versions, and every run directory carries a hash over its scientific content that excludes timestamps and paths. The engine runs headless; this page never runs the model, it reads what the model stored.
