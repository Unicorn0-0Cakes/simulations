Ferry
Terminal

A queue at a ferry terminal is not a queue at a counter. Nothing is served between departures. The berth is empty, vehicles accumulate, and then a whole batch leaves at once — or as much of it as fits.

You set how vehicles arrive, how much deck there is, how often the boat goes, and the order in which the queue is offered to it. Then you have to say who waited, how long, and who never sailed at all. The last group is the difficult one: a vehicle still queueing when the observation ends has not produced a missing measurement, it has produced a lower bound, and dropping it would measure only the vehicles a policy chose to serve.

Methods & limitations

The model family, the evidence behind the arrival mixture, every parameter marked not established, and what this instrument cannot establish.
On the model. This is a bulk-service queue: arrivals accumulate and are served in batches at discrete epochs, in the sense of Bailey's 1954 formulation, with a finite lane-metre capacity rather than a fixed vehicle count. The arrival process is a mixture — one component indifferent to the timetable, one targeting a specific sailing — because the assumption that terminal arrivals are simply Poisson did not survive the evidence. No parameter here is calibrated against a real terminal, and no preset is named after one. What the instrument reproduces is the structure of the problem, not the numbers of any port.
