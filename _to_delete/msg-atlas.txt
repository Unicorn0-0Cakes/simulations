culture-flux: add the atlas entry, backed by a 240-run design

The instrument existed as a headless Python engine with no way into the
catalogue. This adds the three things an entry needs -- an instrument page, a
methods page, and a catalogue record -- and the run data behind them.

Publishing was gated on having something to show. The gate is now cleared:
configs/sweeps/rq2_three_arm.json executed at full replication. 240 runs, 0
failures, 8,786 s of compute. Three mechanisms x K in {1,2,4,10} x 20 seeds,
all sharing a baseline and scenario key so every arm faces a byte-identical
city and byte-identical arrivals.

What it shows, against a compositional baseline of 0.850:

  null (control)        0.8501   sd 0.0000 across all 80 runs
  homophilous copying   0.696-0.789   sd ~0.11
  conformist            0.9999   sd ~0.0001

The two mechanisms disagree about the DIRECTION of the migration effect by
0.30 -- an order of magnitude more than any migration variable moved
anything. And the study's central manipulation does nothing: K=10 against
K=1 gives +0.011 +/- 0.032 and +0.0000. H0-1 not rejected at twenty seeds
per cell. Recorded as pilot note P-10.

The control arm's standard deviation of exactly zero is worth its own line:
that is the compositional arithmetic reproducing itself perfectly, and the
sharpest available check that the seeds, arrivals and city really are matched
across conditions.

Badge choices, deliberately:
- evidence: "Uncalibrated prototype". Section 06 justifies exactly what that
  badge claims -- internally consistent, nothing fitted to data -- and lists
  what is NOT validated, including three open internal-validation targets.
- basis: omitted entirely. That badge names a documented source, and naming
  one here would be false.
- state: "Research preview".

Pre-publish review passes on files, catalogue vocabulary, methods structure,
design-system conformance, offline operation and link integrity. Runtime was
verified headlessly -- every renderer run against the real catalogue and real
data -- rather than in a browser, because the browser pane and the mounted
filesystem are on different hosts.
